# Research: BlackBerry — "the keyboard was a trap"

Research only. No script. Every claim below carries a **S#** tag that resolves to `SOURCES.md`.
Hypothesis tested: *"BlackBerry owned business phones; why the keyboard became a trap."*

**Verdict on the hypothesis: mostly TRUE, with two corrections.**
1. **Correction — BlackBerry was not only a business phone by 2009–10.** It was the **most popular smartphone in America**, period: 43% of US smartphone subscribers in Jan 2010 (S4), best-selling US smartphone brand in calendar 2010 (S3). The trap wasn't "business only" — it was that the **keyboard+secure-email package defined the product** while the rest of the market redefined the product as a computer (S6, S20).
2. **Correction — the keyboard didn't just make the screen small; it made the company confident.** The founders read the iPhone *correctly* ("they've put a Mac in this thing" — S20) and then concluded the corporate core was safe **because** the iPhone couldn't match the keyboard (S20). The keyboard was not a blind spot. It was the calculation.
3. **What the sources do NOT support:** "the keyboard made developers build for it" (no app-count data exists), and "nobody realised the phone was becoming a computer" (Lazaridis realised — S20/S21). Both are cut.

---

## The twist (one sentence)

**Everyone believes BlackBerry was the company that didn't see the iPhone coming — but its founder opened one up, saw "a Mac in this thing" and understood exactly what it was; the keyboard was not a blind spot, it was the reason he bet the company's corporate base was safe, and when the board finally went all-touch in 2013 the market had already put the keyboard at about three percent.** (S20, S21, S24, S32)

Sharper, screenshot-ready version: *"BlackBerry saw the iPhone coming. It just priced the keyboard higher than the computer."*

**Ironic coda (the second twist):** the phones are dead, the company is not. BlackBerry today sells QNX software that runs in **195 million cars** and posted its **record software quarter** in 2025 — a business built on code it bought for **$200 million in 2010** (S19, S34, S16).

## Story outline — 5 chapters

### Ch 1 — The device that made email a place you could be (1984–2007)
- Founded **1984** in Waterloo by Mike Lazaridis and Douglas Fregin; **Jim Balsillie joins in 1992** and turns a science project into a sales machine (S1, S9).
- **BlackBerry introduced 1999** — the first wireless device to sync with corporate email (S2). Push email + QWERTY + it ran through RIM's own network = **an IT department's dream** (S2, S30).
- The lock-in: RIM's FY2013 filing admits IT departments *required* BlackBerrys "including within the U.S. Department of Defense and other government agencies" (S18). That is the moat — and the trap.
- By 2009–10 it is not a niche: **50% of US smartphone sales in Q1 2009** (NPD, three handsets in the top five) and **43% of US smartphone subscribers in Jan 2010** (comScore); best-selling smartphone brand in the US and Canada for calendar 2010 (S4, S3).

### Ch 2 — The peak nobody remembers correctly (FY2011)
- **Fiscal 2011 (to Feb 26 2011): revenue $19.9B, up 33%; 52.3 million phones shipped; net income $3.4B; EPS $6.34** (S3, S37).
- Revenue split for the quarter: **81% devices, 16% service, 3% software** (S3) — the business was *hardware plus a toll on traffic*, not software.
- Subscribers climb to **≈79 million** by Dec 2012 — but the **US base falls 16% in one quarter** while overseas grows (S5). The growth is in cheap markets; the profit was in America.
- The QNX bet is made here: **$200M, April 2010**, to build a next-generation OS (S19).

### Ch 3 — The decision: January 9, 2007
- **iPhone, Jan 9 2007** — Apple sells it as "a widescreen iPod… and a breakthrough Internet communications device," and openly mocks "the small plastic keyboards on many smartphones" (S6).
- Lazaridis's real reaction: "**They've put a Mac in this thing**" and "**If this thing catches on, we're competing with a Mac, not a Nokia**" (S20). The scale of the gap: BlackBerry **one processor, 32 MB**; iPhone OS alone **700 MB** (S21).
- **The trap in one move:** he judged the corporate market safe **because the iPhone couldn't match BlackBerry's keyboard and secure in-house email** (S20).
- The counter-attack is the trap's proof: Verizon asks for an **"iPhone killer" with no keyboard (June 2007)** → the **Storm** (2008), late, "many customers hated it," publicly declared "an overwhelming success" (S22). RIM's first touchscreen was its worst-reviewed phone of the era.
- The PlayBook (April 2011) fails; RIM books a **PlayBook inventory provision** (S7).

### Ch 4 — The fall (Oct 2011 – Dec 2013)
- **Oct 10–13 2011:** an outage across **five continents**; failed switch, failed backups; **$54M** in lost service revenue and penalties (S8). The thing that made BlackBerry reliable publicly wasn't.
- **Jan 22 2012:** the co-CEOs go; **Thorsten Heins** takes over after a year in which RIM lost **three-quarters of its market value** (S9).
- **BB10 launches Jan 30 2013**; RIM becomes BlackBerry; the all-touch **Z10 ships first** (S10). At that launch, BlackBerry's own man puts the keyboard segment at **"only three or so per cent"** of the market (S24).
- **The boardroom scene that *is* the video:** late 2012, Lazaridis holds up a keyboard phone — "**I get this. It's clearly differentiated.**" — then a touchscreen — "**I don't get this.**" He was overruled (S23).
- **Fiscal 2013: revenue $11.1B, down 40%; net loss $628M** (S25). Q4 FY2013 ships **6M phones, only ~1M of them BB10** (S25).
- **Sep 20–27 2013:** revenue ≈**$1.6B**, **$930–960M** inventory charge, **net operating loss $950–995M**, **4,500 jobs cut (~40%)**, "refocus on **enterprise and prosumer**" — i.e. the keyboard's old home (S11).
- **Sep 23 2013:** Fairfax-led LOI at **$9/share, ~$4.7B** — an offer, never a deal (S12).
- **Nov 4 2013:** the buyout dies; a **$1B convertible debenture** replaces it — the filing says "in lieu of finalizing the purchase" (S12). **John Chen** becomes interim CEO, **Heins steps down** (S13).
- **Dec 20 2013:** **Q3 revenue $1,193M; net loss $4.4B; $2.7B impairment; a $1.6B charge "primarily attributable to BlackBerry 10 devices"** (S28). Same day: the **Foxconn** deal — BlackBerry stops building its own phones (S29).

### Ch 5 — Dead phones, living company (2014–today)
- The pivot: **QNX** (bought for **$200M**, S19) becomes the car-software business — QNX is in **195 million vehicles** (S34); QNX automotive + Cylance + licensing become the whole company (S39, S16).
- The phone brand is **sold twice**: TCL (2017, "long-term licensing agreement… the brand and security software") and OnwardMobility (2020–2022, never shipped a device) (S14). Consumer **BBM shut May 31 2019** (S31). **Jan 4 2022: BlackBerry OS devices stop working** (S15).
- The last irony: BlackBerry bought **Cylance for ≈$1.4B in 2019 and sold it for $160M in 2025** (S33) — the same company that once valued its own brand at $4.7B now sells assets in a distressed market and still posts profits: **FY2026 revenue $549.1M**, cash **$432.4M**, QNX at a **record quarter** (S16).
- Ending line candidate: *"The phone died. The keyboard was never the point — the point was being the thing work runs on. BlackBerry just moved, late and painfully, from the desk to the dashboard."* (S34, S16)

## Verify next (uncertain, or Wikipedia-only)

1. **comScore/NPD share figures (S4):** I only have TechCrunch's chart of comScore data and PocketGamer reporting NPD. Find the original comScore communiqué (Jan 2010) or NPD report before putting 43% or 50% on screen.
2. **Globe and Mail insider accounts (S20–S23, S32):** all the best quotes come from one outlet's two investigations (2013, then the 2015 book excerpt). They are credible but single-source; attribute on screen ("according to The Globe and Mail's investigation").
3. **"They've put a Mac in this thing" vs "It's like Apple had stuffed a Mac computer into a cellphone"** — the 2015 book excerpt gives the quote; the 2013 article paraphrases it. Use the 2015 wording and the book attribution.
4. **The Storm "real launch date" and the June 2007 Verizon ask (S22):** the ask date is from the Globe only. The Storm's public launch is usually given as Nov 2008 on Verizon — confirm against a Verizon/AP piece if the date is spoken.
5. **PlayBook inventory provision (S7):** the **$485M** figure comes from Wikipedia; the filing names the provision but I did not extract the number. Get it from the FY2012 6-K clause before saying it.
6. **Workforce figures (S17):** 19,000 → 2,000 cut is Reuters; "20,000 at peak" is Wikipedia. Use "about nineteen thousand" only if Reuters supports it in context.
7. **BBM ~60M (S31):** AllThingsD reporting Heins. Good, but it's a 2013 figure, not a "peak" — never say "peak of 90 million."
8. **Employees today / revenue splits older than FY2026 (S16):** use only the FY2026 10-K numbers (QNX $268M, Secure Comm $258.9M, Licensing $22.2M, total $549.1M).
9. **"195 million vehicles" (S34):** CBC quoting BlackBerry; a company-supplied figure — label it "BlackBerry says."
10. **Stock prices:** the "$148 → $7" framing is unsourced. Either find a wire/source or replace with the sourced "lost three-quarters of its market value in 2011" (S9) and "shares closed down 28% after one earnings miss" (S26).
11. **"BlackBerry owned business phones"** — no sourced share-of-enterprise statistic. Do not put a percentage on it. Use DoD/BYOD language (S18, S30) and the US consumer numbers (S4).
12. **Fiscal-year confusion:** RIM/BlackBerry fiscal years end late Feb/Mar. FY2013 = the year ended Mar 2 2013. Say "the year to March 2013" if the voiceover says a year.
