Sears Didn't Die Online. It Sold Its Own Stores.

(alternatives to test later: The Company That Sold The Roof Over Its Head · Sears Was Forever. Then It Became Its Own Landlord)

======== DESCRIPTION (copy everything between the lines) ========
Everyone says Sears missed the internet. The truth is stranger, and worse for Sears.

In 1984, Sears helped launch Prodigy with IBM and CBS. By 1990 it was the second-largest online service in America. It was in online shopping before the web existed.

What actually ended Sears was the balance sheet. In 2015 it sold 234 of its own stores to Seritage, a company it created, for 2.7 billion dollars. Then it leased those stores back. Sears became a tenant in buildings it had built, paying rent every quarter to the company it had just made.

This is the story of a company that sold its way out of retail, one building at a time.
