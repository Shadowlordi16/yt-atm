# Football second-channel brief

## Recommendation
Build a copyright-safe football channel about **the hidden stories behind clubs**: ownership, managers, transfers, tactics, collapses, rebuilds, and supporter culture. The format is an animated/graphic documentary with original narration, tactical diagrams, timelines, tables, maps, club photos where licensed, and no match-broadcast clips.

Working promise: **“The decision that changed the club.”**

## Why this lane
- YouTube’s own 2025 football trends report describes creator commentary and fan analysis as a major part of football fandom; in its Mexico survey, 66% of sports fans aged 14–49 watched or engaged with creator/fan sports commentary weekly or more. Treat this as directional evidence, not a forecast for every country.
- Football documentary and analysis formats already work on YouTube. Tifo is cited for research-led tactics, club history, and football geopolitics; B/R Football has recently used animated rewrites of club history; Oh My Goal’s “Soccer Stories” is built around untold football stories.
- A documentary angle is more defensible and evergreen than daily rumor/news uploads. It also fits the existing research, script, narration, and motion-graphics pipeline.

## Recommended episode shape
1. Cold open: one decisive moment and a concrete consequence.
2. What supporters usually believe.
3. The hidden decision: a transfer, ownership move, manager choice, or financial constraint.
4. The turning point, shown with tactical diagrams or a timeline.
5. What happened afterward.
6. One sentence the viewer can remember.

Target length: 8–12 minutes for long-form, with two or three original Shorts cut from each episode.

## Visual language
Use a distinct football identity rather than Paper Empire: dark pitch green, cream, white, and one club accent; chalk-board tactics; animated arrows and player dots; transfer timelines; stadium maps; sourced still photographs. Do not use broadcast match footage, official highlight compilations, or copyrighted music.

## First five pilot topics
1. **How Manchester United Lost Its Identity After Ferguson** — a decision-and-structure story, not a match recap.
2. **The Transfer That Broke Barcelona’s Rebuild** — focus on finances, recruitment, and squad planning; verify every claim against filings and reputable reporting.
3. **How Leicester Built a Premier League Champion** — recruitment, scouting, coaching, and the 2015–16 system.
4. **Why Valencia Fell From European Power to Crisis Club** — ownership, debt, stadium decisions, and sporting decline.
5. **How Napoli Turned a Scudetto Squad Into a Modern Club** — recruitment, coaching, and the post-title transition.

## Best first episode
Start with **Leicester’s 2015–16 title**, if the research supports a clean source trail. It has immediate recognition, a strong underdog hook, tactical visuals, a finite story, and a positive ending. If current sources are easier for a different club, use a recent documented collapse with primary financial or court records.

## Copyright and monetization rules
- Match footage is not a safe foundation. YouTube explains that fair use is case-specific and does not prevent claims; rights holders can still issue claims or takedowns.
- Use original graphics, diagrams, public-domain or licensed photos, club press images only where permission/license is clear, and short quoted material with attribution.
- Every episode must add original analysis and narration. Avoid reading articles, reposting fan clips, or making interchangeable transfer-news videos.

## Test plan
Release five videos over four weeks. Compare click-through rate, first-30-second retention, average percentage viewed, subscribers per 1,000 views, and comments. Keep the winning story type (club collapse, tactical innovation, transfer, or underdog rise) and drop the weakest format.
