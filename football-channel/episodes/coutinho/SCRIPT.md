## CHAPTER 1: HOW A FEE WORKS

> Here is the thing football never explains. A transfer fee is not a bill. It is a balance-sheet entry. [S3]

> Barca pay one hundred and forty-two million, and then spread that cost across the length of the contract. [S3]

> In accounting, it is called amortisation. In football, it is how a huge fee becomes a small annual number. [S3]

> A five-year contract turns one hundred and forty-two million into about twenty-eight million a year. [S3]
- Chart: the 5 bars.

> That number sits on the books whether he plays or not. [S3]

## CHAPTER 2: WHAT THEY ALREADY HAD

> So why buy him? Because Barcelona were not buying a footballer. They were buying an answer to a question. [S4]

> Neymar had left for Paris that summer, for a world-record two hundred and twenty-two million euros. [S4]

> Barca had the money and a hole in the left side of the pitch. Coutinho was the replacement. [S4]

> The problem: he was not a winger. He was a number ten who wanted to play through the middle. [S5]

> Liverpool knew exactly where to use him. Left, cutting in, arriving late. It worked because the team was built for it. [S5]

> Barcelona's middle already had Messi. There was no room in the one place he was best. [S5]
- Tactical pitch diagram: Coutinho's zones vs Messi's.

> So Barca played him wide, in a system designed for somebody else, in a language he did not yet speak. [S5]

> He was not bad. He was a good player in the wrong rectangle. [S5]

> The club that bought him watched their record signing demolish them, and then watched Bayern win the treble. [S6]

## CHAPTER 4: THE PRICE OF THE HOLE

> But the real damage was happening away from the pitch. [S7]

> Barcelona's wage bill had grown to more than five hundred million euros a year. The highest in football. [S7]

> Here is the mechanism, and it is the whole story. In Spain, your wage bill is tied to your revenue. [S7]

> Spend future revenue on today's players, and next year's limit falls. The club gets poorer by getting bigger. [S7]
- Diagram: revenue in, limit out.

> They had spent the Neymar money on three players: Coutinho, Dembele, Griezmann. None worked. [S7][S4]

> Under La Liga's salary rules, the club could not register new players. They could not even register Messi. [S7]

> Messi left in 2021, for free. The greatest player of his generation, on a free transfer. [S8]

> So the Coutinho deal was not the cause. It was the test case. The transfer that proved Barcelona's model was spending future money on present answers. [S3][S7]

> And that is why this is not really a story about Coutinho. He was the invoice. The problem was the policy. [S3]
